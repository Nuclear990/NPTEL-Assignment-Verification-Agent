## Traditional Research Areas and Department Structure

Our department is structured into three groups: a Fluid Mechanics group, a Solid Mechanics group, and a Bio‑Mechanics and Biomedical Engineering group. Within these groups we have many more granular sub‑areas, reflecting a high degree of interdisciplinarity. Faculty come from eight different undergraduate backgrounds—physicists, aerospace engineers, mechanical engineers, civil engineers, and others—creating a melting pot of sciences and engineering that gives our research a distinctive flavor.  

Classically, the department has focused on mechanics‑related problems in both fluid and solid mechanics. More recently, faculty have begun emphasizing fluid‑structure interaction, cell mechanics (a bio‑mechanics area interfacing with solid mechanics), and the integration of electrical engineering with mechanical engineering. Even within traditional domains, research pushes boundaries, so leading‑edge work is always present.

## Emerging Research Areas and Technological Advances

Two technological advances have enabled many newer research areas worldwide: (1) extremely fast computational tools, and (2) sophisticated analysis and visualization tools such as electron microscopy. Faculty now conduct experiments and simulations using these tools. For example, some researchers study tissue properties with nano‑indentation and atomic force microscopy, while others perform high‑fidelity computations on industry‑scale combustion problems using direct numerical simulation of complex flows. These challenging problems have become tractable thanks to the new tools, and the insights they generate are expected to lead to industry‑scale deployments in the near future.  

## India‑Focused Research and Application

In addition to emerging work, we must note a distinctive aspect of research at IIT Madras: many India‑specific problems can be solved with traditionally developed tools. Organizations such as DRDO, ISRO, public‑sector undertakings, and rural development initiatives benefit from solutions that do not require ultra‑sophisticated equipment but do require “India‑scale” thinking. We are not ready to discard these traditional solutions because they are well suited to our national challenges.

## Student Transition to Research and Challenges

Moving from a classroom degree program to a research program is a major step in a scholar’s journey. In a classroom, problems are well defined—e.g., “given three variables, find the fourth.” In research, you may be given only one variable and must determine the other three while justifying your assumptions. Defining one’s own research area within the faculty’s interests and available resources is the second major challenge.  

Our faculty typically grant scholars the freedom to choose their research topics within the boundaries of faculty expertise and available equipment. These challenges are positive; they foster growth. The key is to remain open‑minded, view challenges as opportunities, and use the resources and peer group available. Our department’s student body is highly diversified, so discussing problems with peers often reveals viable solutions.

## Industry Collaboration and Relevance

As a publicly funded institution, we have a mandate to serve Indian industry. Many faculty members are actively involved with DRDO, ISRO, and other agencies, serving on expert committees and participating in specific projects. This provides direct industry engagement for both faculty and students. Indirectly, we work on industry‑related problems that serve as training rounds for scholars, preparing them for employment after graduation.  

Our graduates find positions in Indian industry—companies such as GE, Boeing, and Mercedes‑Benz regularly recruit our research scholars because they have been trained on industry‑scale problems during their time in the department.

## Measuring Success and Student Development

Observing student growth is truly phenomenal. New students often experience a dramatic increase in confidence in their abilities, a confidence that persists throughout their careers. In addition to confidence, students acquire a broad skill set through exposure to world‑class infrastructure, a strong peer group, and diverse faculty expertise. Over the first few years, they sample many techniques and become well‑equipped. In the final two years of a typical Ph.D., confidence grows exponentially, evident in committee presentations and overall research maturity. Watching this progression is rewarding for faculty and beneficial for the students as they transition beyond IIT.

## Career Paths and Placement for Graduates

Placement for Ph.D. graduates differs markedly from undergraduate placement. While many graduates secure industry positions that directly leverage their acquired skills, a substantial fraction—approximately a quarter—enter roles that are not directly related but where their background enables rapid adaptation.  

The largest employer of Ph.D. graduates in India is academia. With about 16 IITs (and more to be added), numerous NITs, state institutions, and a growing number of private universities, there is a strong demand for faculty members. Many of our scholars become faculty elsewhere, bringing back the training they received here.  

We encourage every Ph.D. graduate to consider an academic career, as India faces a massive upcoming demand for qualified teachers. Over the next 10–15 years, the number of young men and women entering colleges will be unprecedented, and Ph.D. graduates from institutions like the IITs will be essential to meet this training challenge.

## Advisor‑Student Interaction Frequency

Initially, a fairly high frequency of meetings is advisable so that advisor and student can get to know each other. Over time, each advisor‑student pair typically settles into a comfortable rhythm. Many faculty hold regular weekly group meetings and may meet more frequently with individual students. Some faculty, once a scholar has gained sufficient confidence, may suggest that the student work more independently and meet less often than weekly.
It depends entirely on the advisor and the student, but you can define the level of interaction together. You simply sit down with your advisor and decide what frequency is appropriate for you. We also have a very thriving external program, where meetings are less frequent but equally productive in terms of research.

At IIT we are now a predominantly postgraduate institution, and the Department of Applied Mechanics is a postgraduate‑only department. Conflicts—whether student‑student or advisor‑student—do arise, but they should not unnerve you. Professional mechanisms are in place to handle conflicts at all levels, and we expect everyone to act professionally. I do not expect to be liked by every person on the planet; if a conflict occurs, we have a process to resolve it professionally, move on, and even remain friends.

IIT takes pride in this approach. Although occasional issues between guides and students occur, the established mechanisms ensure that students graduate happy, even after navigating such difficulties. In fact, only a minuscule fraction of the roughly 2,000 PhD students on campus encounter serious problems.

If you are apprehensive about selecting a guide, do not be fearful. If a particular arrangement does not work out, there are many other options available. Approach the process with an open mind, and you will likely find a suitable fit.

**Advice for aspiring Applied Mechanics students**

- Come in with an open mind and be ready to do interdisciplinary work; today’s problems rarely fit into vertical silos. A mechanical engineering background alone is seldom sufficient.
- Enjoy the ride. Research is a walk through uncharted territory for anyone, anywhere in the world.
- Success is recognized when peers outside IIT acknowledge your work—through publications, a pat on the back after a conference presentation, or other forms of recognition.
- Remember that your peer group is not just the ten students around you; it is the global community. That is why we publish in international venues.
- Aim to publish in the best channels you can. Doing so will set you on a solid path for the rest of your career.
